The Way In

A Growth Gap Analysis that puts a number on every gap, then a free site with a plan.

Two entry points, both built to convert into the retainer rather than end at a single invoice.

Entry point one

The Growth Gap Analysis

A diagnostic that maps where they lose work today and prices each gap: where they do not show up in search, where they are missing from AI answers, where admin eats hours. Framed on the cost of doing nothing, not on features. Every gap carries a number.

Concrete, not vague. "This search gap costs about £14,700 a month in work you never see." "This automation saves about 47 hours a month off your team." The prospect should leave certain that not fixing it costs more than hiring us.

Priced by who is buying. At the small end (Foundation and Growth) it is free or a light fee: a live mockup of their refreshed site plus an Authority Engine snapshot showing where they do and do not appear in AI answers. Results in advance, given before any money changes hands. At the regulated end (Operator and Authority) it is a paid diagnostic at £3,000 to £5,000, where the depth and the compliance mapping carry the cost, set against what the gap is costing them every month.

Why it works: a quantified business case, not a sales pitch. The prospect sees the monthly cost of the gap next to the monthly fee that closes it, so the retainer is the obvious next step. A buyer who has paid for the diagnosis has already said yes once.
Entry point two

A free website with a retained plan

The site is free on every tier, built on our managed CMS so nothing is one-off. A 12-month term is the standard and recovers the build cost cleanly, though we can flex to a 3 or 6-month term per client. An early-exit buyout covers it if they leave sooner. Fair both ways.

This is the website-as-a-service model: no capital outlay for them, predictable monthly revenue for us, and the site never leaves the CMS.

The line for the page: the website is on us, you stay because it works, the standard term is 12 months so we recover the build, flexible to 3 or 6 months if a client needs shorter.

The Guarantee

A stack of guarantees a regulated buyer respects. Not a gamble on the market.

Structured and conditional, every one. Confidence shown without exposure to outcomes nobody controls, and not one of them tied to a percentage of revenue.

1

Results in advance

Before any money changes hands, a real artifact: a live mockup of their refreshed site, or an Authority Engine audit showing where they do and do not appear in AI answers today. The move that made three live sites land as "gold."

2

Within the first 45 days we identify and present at least 10x our monthly fee in documented revenue opportunities, efficiency gains, marketing waste cut, or automation savings. If we do not, we keep working at no charge until we do.

3

An SLA credit, billable-backed

A number on responsiveness and delivery. Miss an agreed response window or a deliverable date and you get a credit against next month. An SLA, not a promise about the market.

4

A Compliance and Governance SLA

Every piece of AI content and every automation holds to regulatory compliance, non-hallucinated citations checked against source, and secure data routing per client. Written into the agreement, not assumed.

5

An hours-saved guarantee, optional

On an automation build, we can put a number on the hours it removes each month and stand behind it. If it does not hit the figure, we keep tuning it at no charge until it does.

6

A 30-day get-out

No long handcuff on the service, build cost aside. The moat does the retention, not the contract.

Stated plainly

The fee is not tied to the client's revenue or to lead volume. In regulated sectors that structure is a legal trap, the kind that triggers anti-kickback and marketing-rule exposure. Flat, fair-value, labour-based pricing only. This is the part James will be looking for.

Decisions for James and Antony

Considerations: open commercial questions.

Three calls left open on purpose. We have pressure-tested each against 2026 market data, here is the thinking, and these are the ones for us to land together.

A

Foundation at £499, keep it or guard it harder.

The case for keeping it: £499 is the low-barrier door that gets a fast yes at low risk, then the ladder does the rest. It is fully productised already, no bespoke work and no calls beyond onboarding, so it costs almost nothing to deliver. The known risk, and the market is clear on this, is that the lowest tier draws the churn-prone, support-heavy buyer, the one who pays £499 and emails every week. The productised guardrails are how that gets contained: a fixed scope, email-only support, self-serve editing under the CMS, no custom work at this tier. So the cheap entry stays cheap to run.

Open question

Keep Foundation at £499 as is, or tighten the guardrails further before we open it to volume.

B

Contract term, flexible from 3 to 12 months.

We can run the term at 3, 6 or 12 months, and a 12-month term works best: it recovers the free build cleanly and gives us the strongest, most predictable recurring base. The case for a shorter 3 or 6-month option is friction at signing. The 2026 market is drifting off long lock-ins, and the CMS and the control centre are the real lock-in anyway, not the paper, so a shorter term can win the cautious or regulated buyer who is slower to commit to a long tie, exactly Antony's lane. A shorter term just leans more on the early-exit buyout to carry the build cost.

Open question

Lead with the 12-month term as standard (cleanest build recovery, strongest recurring), offer 3 or 6-month options where a client needs less commitment, or flex per client. Our lean is 12-month as the default.

C

Pricing at the top, are Operator and Authority underpriced for regulated work.

2026 benchmarks put regulated and enterprise AI work at £5,000 to £10,000+ a month. Against that, Operator at £2,500 and Authority from £3,999 may be light for a regulated client, where the compliance mapping, the governance SLA and the premium Authority Engine layers carry real cost and real value. The case for a higher band: a rail or BAE-grade buyer reads £2,500 as a small-vendor price, and the premium layers (LLM seeding, GEO, cross-jurisdiction audit mapping) are what justify a step up. The case for holding: the current ladder lands accounts, and per-seat expansion already lifts the top tiers well past the flat number.

Open question

Add a regulated / enterprise band from £5k a month, captured via the premium Authority Engine layers, or hold the current ladder and let per-seat do the lifting.

Why these are open and the rest is not. Every figure elsewhere in this brief is anchored and ready to run. These three are the genuine forks where the data points more than one way, so they are calls for the two of us, made on the evidence above.
