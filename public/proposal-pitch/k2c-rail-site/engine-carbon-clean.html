How it works

Hydrogen, injected through the air intake. Six-cycle service. No teardown.

The cleaning process runs in-service across a 60 to 90 minute window. No engine teardown, no fleet downtime beyond a normal maintenance slot. The hydrogen reaction lifts deposited carbon from piston crowns, valve seats, and injector tips. Residue exits as standard combustion product.

Stage 1 · Baseline measurement

Fuel burn rate, exhaust opacity, and turbo back-pressure measured against the unit’s service-record baseline. This is what every reduction figure is measured against.

Stage 2 · Hydrogen injection cycle

Controlled hydrogen flow injected through the air intake at engine idle. Six controlled cycles across 60 to 90 minutes depending on engine displacement. Operator stays trackside throughout, full operational supervision.

Stage 3 · Post-service validation

Repeat measurement of fuel burn, exhaust opacity, and back-pressure. Delta written to the unit’s service record and uploaded to the K2C ECC dashboard for CP7 reporting evidence.

Stage 4 · Quarterly re-baseline

Standard re-measurement at the next quarterly service. Most fleets see partial drift back toward baseline at 12-18 months, scheduled cleaning becomes part of the maintenance regime.
