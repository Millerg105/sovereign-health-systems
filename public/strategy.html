03Holes in the Plan (poked properly)

Things that look fine on paper but will bite you within 90 days if you don't address them now.

1. Tenant isolation in shared Supabase

High

Both brands' clients land in the same database. Without strict Row Level Security (RLS) and a brand column on every table, a Bookings client could theoretically query Systems client data, or your dashboard could leak the wrong rows. This is a GDPR landmine.

Fix: add a brand enum column ('systems' | 'bookings') to every shared table. Enforce RLS policies that filter by brand AND user. Audit before onboarding client #2 on either side.

2. Pricing logic conflict (free site vs paid site)

High

Systems sells websites for £500 to £2k. Bookings gives the same site away free as a hook. If a Systems prospect ever finds out a Bookings client got an equivalent site for nothing, you've trained the market that your paid price is fictional.

Fix: Bookings free sites are explicitly NICHE LANDING PAGES (single-page, no CMS, no edits). Systems sites are full multi-page builds. Different SKUs, different scope. Document the difference publicly so it's defensible.

3. Free site delivery cost not tracked

High

You're giving away a £3-5k asset to win a £500 setup + £300/show deal. If it takes you 12 hours to ship and the client churns at month 2 having booked 4 appointments, you've lost money to acquire them.

Fix: log delivery hours per Bookings client. Set a cap: max 8 hours total build using Tranquil template + niche swap. If a build creeps past that, it's a Systems job and gets quoted separately. Review unit economics monthly.

4. Email signature routing is fragile

Med

One Gmail handles both brands. Joe's reply to a Bookings prospect could land with a Systems signature if he's in the wrong template. First impression damage is permanent.

Fix: Gmail labels per brand + canned response templates per brand. OR: dedicated alias (joe@sovereignbookings.co.uk) that auto-fills the right signature. Set up before Joe's outreach scales past 50 sends/day.

5. Tranquil template re-use = visible IP

Med

Every garden room niche site looks identical because they're all forks of Tranquil. A prospect who sees two of your client sites side by side spots the trick. Trust drops.

Fix: 3 colour palettes + 3 hero layout variations baked into the template. Vary section ordering. Different testimonial format per client. Take 30 mins per build to differentiate. Worth it for trust.

6. Brand confusion in shared dashboard

Med

Same dashboard codebase serves both brands. A Systems client logs in and sees "Sovereign Bookings" branding because we forgot to theme it. Looks unprofessional, raises pricing questions.

Fix: brand prop on session. Logo, colours, and copy switch automatically based on which subdomain the user signed in from. dashboard.sovereignsystem.co.uk vs dashboard.sovereignbookings.co.uk. One codebase, two skins.

7. Pivoting churns the team

Med

You and Joe pivoted twice in 6 weeks (Systems → Bookings → both). Bolting on driveways, roofing, SaaS, or white-label before garden rooms is proven burns 12 months on positioning instead of selling. Garden rooms ONLY inside Bookings until 3 paying clients + 30 days clean data.

Fix: hard rule. No new sub-niche under Bookings until garden rooms hits 3 paying clients with 30 days of clean data. No third brand under Sovereign Digital until combined MRR is £10k+. Write this on the wall.

8. SEO cannibalisation between sites

Low

Both sites talk about "lead generation for trades". They'll compete for the same keywords on Google. One outranks the other and the loser becomes invisible.

Fix: Systems goes after "web design [city]" and "marketing automation [city]". Bookings goes after "[trade] leads [city]" and "qualified appointments". Keyword maps documented before any blog content goes live.

9. Accountant clarity on revenue split

Low

Stripe receipts say "SOVEREIGN BOOKINGS" regardless of which brand sold the deal. At year-end, separating Systems revenue from Bookings revenue for management accounts becomes guesswork.

Fix: Stripe metadata on every invoice/payment link: brand: systems | bookings. Notion Client Accounts DB already has the column structure for this. Tag at point of sale, not in retrospect.

10. Joe's split focus

Low

Joe sells two different offers with two different scripts to two different audiences. His brain will default to the easier sell (probably Bookings) and Systems pipeline dies quietly.

Fix: Joe is BOOKINGS-FIRST until £5k MRR. Systems leads route to Miller for now. Once Bookings is producing predictable revenue, train Joe on Systems pitch as second motion. Don't split his time before then.

04Decision Rules (when in doubt)

These are the bright-line rules. Any time you can't decide which brand a thing belongs to, run it through these.

Lead came in

Niche trade asking for leads → Bookings

Garden rooms only for now. PPA pitch. Free site as hook. Other trades (driveways, roofing, paving) parked until garden rooms is proven.

Lead came in

Anyone else asking for a website → Systems

Plumbers, accountants, gyms, restaurants. Quote site + retainer.

New offer

Performance-based pricing → Bookings

Pay per show, pay per booking, pay per result. Always Bookings.

New offer

Fixed-price deliverable → Systems

Site, automation build, custom dashboard, integration. Always Systems.

Sub-niche test

Same template works → landing page under Bookings

Niche fits PPA model + can be Tranquil-forked in under 8 hours. New page, not new brand.

Sub-niche test

Needs different template + sales motion → wait

If it doesn't fit Tranquil, hold it until Bookings hits £10k MRR. Then revisit as own brand.

07The One-Sentence Test

Print this and stick it on the wall. Anytime you're tempted to start a new brand, niche, or offer, run it through here.

"Will this make Sovereign Digital Ltd more money in the next 90 days, or am I just bored?"

Discipline

If the answer is "I'm bored," close the tab. The productive avoidance pattern is the biggest risk to this whole plan. Two brands done well beats five brands half-built.
